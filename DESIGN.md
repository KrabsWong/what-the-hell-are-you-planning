# Task Manager interaction design

## Direction

Keep the existing native workspace layout, system fonts and warm neutral palette. Use rounded surfaces with native glass on macOS 26+ and opaque fallbacks, with a continuous sidebar/titlebar backdrop and an inset main workspace. Light mode serves daytime reading and dark mode serves dim environments; both must keep action labels and icons visible at rest. Default pages serve non-developer research workflows, with technical configuration and diagnostics on demand. Preserve the existing palette, button discoverability and all execution capabilities.

## Components

- Primary: solid deep warm fill (solid light warm fill in dark mode) for create, generate, save and run. Emphasize one advancing action per local action group.
- Secondary: use the main-branch ghost style for supporting actions such as settings, edit, copy, close or back. Labels remain visible, with hover and focus feedback instead of a permanent button border. `controls::quiet_button` is the standard; `controls::row_button` is for compound surfaces: sidebar/tree rows, board cards, popup menu rows and segmented controls. Task tabs keep their own shared-border rule below.
- Action hierarchy: reserve the primary fill for the one advancing action in a local group. Use secondary controls for frequent supporting actions, direct Lucide icon controls for compact well-understood actions, and a More (…) menu for suitable low-frequency actions. Do not render every action as an equally prominent button, and do not flatten everything to ghost styling either. Each icon/More trigger keeps a full hit area, tooltip and meaningful accessibility label, and its destination or state must stay unambiguous.
- Layout switch: List/Board uses the library `TabBar::segmented()` with its built-in spring-driven selection background and reduced-motion policy. Preserve icon-plus-label content and the separate collection/step layout state.
- Selection: bordered selection control; the selected state uses an accent surface/text and a clear visual change, and also sets the toggled semantic state. `controls::select_button` owns this appearance; selection never borrows the primary fill. Selection stays distinct from the one advancing action in the group.
- Link: underlined accent text for navigation, sources and opening files; body-text URLs follow the same rule. Settings actions remain buttons.
- Icon: display the Lucide icon directly without a permanent button surface or border, matching main. `controls::tool_icon` retains a fixed square hit area, hover and focus feedback, tooltip and accessibility_label; never substitute a bare text × or +. The same control may host a More menu for low-frequency actions, but must not hide the page's main action or an important disabled reason.
- Task tab: the title and close icon share one background and border, with no separate button border inside. The close icon is always visible and keeps its own hit area, hover and focus feedback. Long titles ellipsize to the available width, reveal the full name on hover, and never compress the close area.
- Danger: red outline and tint via `controls::danger_button` for stopping an active run, cancelling an active period or removing material. Ordinary Close or Cancel uses the secondary control, not danger color.
- Disabled: keep the shape, weaken fill and text, and explain the business reason near the button; never trigger the action.
- Focus: keep the component library's focus drawing and Tab/Enter/Space semantics; hover alone is insufficient.

## Geometry and typography

Action buttons are about 32 px tall with 8 px radius and 14 px system font; Undo in the bottom status bar uses a compact 24 px button. Toolbars stay on one line and truncate long titles instead of compressing buttons. Horizontally scrollable boards keep their current column widths. Form/detail action rows may wrap. Sidebar content scrolls and the theme switch stays fixed at the bottom instead of being pushed out as projects grow.

Geometry comes from `appearance.rs`: controls use 8 px, content groups use 12 px, major content surfaces use 16 px, and floating dialogs/search use 20 px. The main workspace has a 10 px inset beside and below its content surface, below the navigation header. A 6 px inner gutter keeps rectangular child painting inside the curved perimeter because GPUI overflow masks are rectangular. This shell applies to every route, including settings, automations, research, results, recovery and archive. Onboarding uses the same surface vocabulary outside the main shell. Keep compact passive tags and circular indicators distinct from panels.

Platforms without native glass and Reduce Transparency use opaque surfaces. The sidebar, titlebar and footer share the sidebar background; content uses the existing canvas and panel tones. The main workspace casts a soft leading-edge shadow onto the sidebar, replacing the full-height divider with a layered edge. The library resize target and cursor remain, with no additional line or grip during hover or drag. Keep the leading shadow narrow and low contrast so it does not read as a full-height strip. Other soft layered shadows belong to floating dialogs and menus, with subtle borders for dark-mode separation. Application-owned dialogs share `floating_surface`; library controls and menus use the same theme geometry. Report tables retain functional row and column dividers. Collection group backgrounds and row hover highlights use the 8 px control radius, with 4 px between rows instead of horizontal rules that intersect hover highlights; preserve content heights and column alignment. Do not turn every row into a floating card. Preserve retained rendering boundaries, fixed row heights, focus feedback and nested scrolling. Clip the main content surface without clipping the root's dialogs or their shadows.

Liquid Glass is a persisted, default-off setting under General. The switch is disabled with a reason on unsupported systems, unavailable native windows, or while Reduce Transparency is enabled. A saved on preference is preserved during temporary accessibility restrictions, but the material is inactive. On supported macOS systems, normal startup prepares native glass and shows it only when enabled: one continuous regular-style `NSGlassEffectView` sits below the GPUI Metal view, with a clear native window background and no internal rounded seams. The sidebar, top navigation header, bottom status bar and workspace gutters expose this continuous material. The rounded content panel uses a dense translucent wash over the same native material (55% neutral charcoal over a single 60% dark shell wash in dark mode, 55% neutral white over a single 60% white shell wash in light mode). Dark glass cards and controls use coordinated neutral grays with brighter secondary text; light controls retain their existing colors while the reading surface uses neutral frosted white without a yellow-green tint. Existing nested cards and dialogs retain their opaque fills; no rectangular backing fills the panel’s outside corners. Regular material preserves foreground readability in the active window. Native glass is unavailable before macOS 26 and falls back to the original opaque shell and content colors when Reduce Transparency is enabled. The isolated QA preview uses the same material and colors with sample data.

Settings > General places a compact **Content → Output language** dropdown before Appearance. **Match request** is the default; selecting a language overrides generated summaries, plans, task instructions and reports across Research and Automations. The value saves immediately and remains outside model profiles. User-authored text, quoted source text, URLs, JSON keys and protocol enum values are never translated.

Opening an automation result prepares the selected period's saved result bodies asynchronously before revealing the dialog. A newer selection replaces the pending destination, and navigation cancels it. Failed reads open the existing error/retry view. Cached results open immediately. Automation result dialogs keep short reports in the natural outer scroll flow. Reports exceeding 4 KiB or 40 lines use a bounded reading area with a vertical scrollbar and block virtualization. Its height follows the window (45% of viewport height, clamped to 180–420 px). A partially clipped reader is brought into view before its text scrolls. Vertical input over the article moves only the article while it can scroll; subsequent input at an edge moves the outer dialog. Metadata, sources and research steps remain in the outer flow. Horizontal input, text selection, links and scrollbar dragging retain their normal behavior.

The sidebar brand mark and app name stay fixed above the navigation scroll area. Scrolling moves only the navigation list; the workspace switcher stays fixed below it, separated by whitespace rather than a horizontal rule or another shadow.

Result headers keep the title and actions on one line. Actions stay right-aligned without wrapping; long titles ellipsize and expose the full title on hover. Result kind, run, model, version and source metadata occupy a separate row below and may wrap independently.

Report readers fill the available content width and reflow when the window or navigation sidebar changes size. Final reports lead with the answer and reasoning. A recognized Appendix and the structured Sources list default to collapsed disclosures after the report body in both native readers and published HTML; expanding them never hides or rewrites the answer, and copy/export retain the complete report. Task details use a flexible body beside a fixed 240 px Task properties column, matching the main-branch layout; the properties column does not expand or stack below the body.

## Workspace identity and status bar

- Workspace identity is fixed at the bottom of the sidebar on the same row as the theme switch and replaces the standalone Settings nav item: name, local-storage icon and a dropdown menu. The menu offers Reveal data folder, Copy data folder path and Settings…; the storage location, the backup entry point and the Settings page all start here. The row shows the selected state while Settings is open. Its chevron points down when the menu is closed and up when open, rotating over 180 ms from the menu's actual open state. Interrupted transitions reverse smoothly; reduced motion switches directly to the target orientation.
- The left side of the status bar reports only persistence state and the latest action feedback. Saved uses a neutral check; writing uses the accent dot with "Saving changes…"; a failed save uses the danger tone with the reason and a Retry save action; unavailable storage explains that changes are only in memory.
- The right side of the status bar holds only real commands: Undo, Search, New research. Shortcuts are stated in tooltips (⌘K / ⌘N) instead of rendering shortcuts as non-clickable body text. A disabled Undo explains why in its tooltip: nothing to undo, submitted work must finish first, or automation changes are protected from whole-store rollback.

## Boundaries

Completed step board cards use the check icon once, without a repeated Done label or generic completion sentence. Keep the status in the card's tooltip and accessible name. Preserve distinct context such as archival labels and specific reasons, and keep explanatory text for unfinished states.

Non-interactive chips, task statuses and field labels keep low contrast and no button border. Clickable lists/cards need hover feedback and a visible open arrow or action; do not give body copy a button shape. Goal list rows show their ID as plain text, keep priority in the ID tooltip, and omit a leading priority icon or trailing ID chevron. The `STATUS` column is the single status source in list rows, using the shortest execution label such as `Paused` or `Done` and the shared semantic status color; do not repeat that state as an icon before the title. Paused projects and Goal result step rows use only the leading status icon and preserve the written state in the row’s accessible label and hover text, without a second status label at the right edge. Step progress is quiet text such as `9/9 steps`, with no permanent border or arrow; it remains a compact direct control for opening Steps while the whole row remains the primary task target. Sidebar chevrons mean disclosure only: rows that expand on click (projects, topics, goals) keep a chevron, while plain navigation rows show only their count. Shortcut navigation keeps the sidebar structure. In All research and Archive, the goal completion count is a compact navigation control with a fixed right chevron: it opens that goal’s Steps tab. It does not expand inline children. Back restores the originating collection’s filter and scroll position.

Run context selectors follow one rule: only fields with multiple choices keep a dropdown caret and menu. A single Project, Topic or Goal renders as the same-sized read-only value without hover or a caret. Copied Topics show the compact `Copy` tag inline before the title in both the selected value and menu rows, matching the sidebar.

Completed task details show the written status only in the **Task → Status** property. The execution panel above it keeps Run again, Open goal and Technical details, but omits a second Done heading and generic completion sentence. Opening a task from a run result page, including a progress row, Goal details or Open task & resolve, is a drill-down, so Back restores the same run result and selected run. When Technical details is open, the global Back action and ⌘[ return to the originating result view before any route-level Back.

Completed report regeneration keeps upstream inputs unchanged. If the saved report's input signature is stale, the regeneration editor shows inline that later input changes are not included, while still allowing a rewrite from the selected report's saved evidence snapshot.

Automation history and result rows have 8 px of separation and keep one neutral surface and express state through semantic status text and accents rather than a large row fill. Trigger source shows a compact Manual/Scheduled text label beside its icon, with skipped-period detail in the tooltip. Long automation names and step titles ellipsize but keep the complete value in the tooltip and accessibility label. The header contains Back and the location path only; it has no Recently opened menu. Search results use compact two-line rows: a single-line title above the type, location/status and destination. Ellipsize long text within the available width, preserve full accessible labels, and reserve the selection border space to avoid layout shifts.

Readable text must be drag-selectable and copyable with ⌘C: body copy, titles, descriptions, reports, setting values, automation summaries and history all render through `views::selectable` (reports use TextView's built-in selection). Button text, navigation items and compact labels manually ellipsized on canvas do not participate in selection, preserving click semantics and truncation accuracy.

## Scrolling acceptance

Actual scrolling smoothness on the isolated native application is mandatory and is defined by the authoritative [scrolling acceptance contract](doc/review-2026-11-08/TASKS.md#mandatory-scrolling-acceptance). Static screenshots, averages or synthetic slices do not satisfy it. Under the first-round acceptance update, occasional isolated hitches may be documented and deferred; repeated or sustained stalls and incorrect scrolling remain failures.

## Source of truth

`appearance.rs` owns theme and component colors. `controls.rs` owns button/link factories. Pages choose action semantics and bind behavior only; auxiliary and icon controls follow the main-branch ghost styling while primary, selection and destructive actions retain their distinct styles. Strike the hierarchy from one advancing primary action per local group down to secondary, icon and More actions; verify it in both themes. Native screenshots and interaction acceptance cover 1440×920, 1000×680 and both light and dark themes.

## Model settings

The global model entry sits at the bottom of the sidebar. The global page combines the default model, an independently searchable local model list, save actions and the current override list. An empty stage input inherits the global setting, and the field shows the effective model and its source; clearing an override takes effect only after saving. "Use this model for all stages" on the global page is an explicit option; saving never starts a task or changes history. Project planning windows and task details show the same effective-model rules.

When every stage inherits, the checkbox stays checked according to the actual configuration and acts as a read-only state; changing one stage override allows unifying again. Save feedback sits in a dedicated area at the top of the main content and never floats over edit buttons. Success and failure combine an icon, title and explanation; On the live v4 path, Saving remains visible while the ordered worker has an outstanding write; Saved appears only after the matching durable acknowledgement. A failed save retains its retryable intent and never claims success. Legacy/offline synchronous paths report their actual save result. Final runtime and performance acceptance remains tracked in the review documents. Feedback can be dismissed, and changing pages or abandoning a form clears the old message.

Inline notices use semantic tones: success green, warning orange, error red, neutral information gray. Each notice is a rounded box with a tinted surface: the background blends the tone with the panel color, and the border and text use the same tone; avoid weak "white fill + colored outline" styling. All colors come from `Palette`; dark mode automatically uses the dark panel with the same semantic color, and hardcoded light backgrounds are forbidden.

## Sidebar order

Paused projects lists only current, unsuperseded recovery work. A planning attempt that was interrupted but later replaced by a newer attempt for the same request, or whose request already produced goals, remains history and is not shown as paused work. Current interrupted planning requests offer direct **Retry planning** or **Continue understanding** together with an **Open topic** or **Open project** destination where the request can be reviewed, replanned or archived.

The sidebar presents two domains in this order: **Automations** (All automations, Automation results) above **Research** (All research, projects, Paused projects, Research results, Archive). Archive belongs to the Research group rather than sitting as an unlabelled bottom row, so a long sidebar always makes its scope clear.

## Automations

The maintained current/target route and action contract is [doc/ROUTE_ACTION_MATRIX.md](doc/ROUTE_ACTION_MATRIX.md); this section records the visual and interaction design.

- The sidebar separates **Research** and **Automations**. Research contains **All research** (all non-automation root tasks/goals), the project list, **Paused projects**, **Research results** and **Archive** for archived research only. Automations is a separate section containing **All automations** (the automation list) and **Automation results** (all automation period results) directly, not under Workspace alongside All, and with no expandable second-level parent. Automation results are fully separate from Research results; the latter contains only project/topic/goal research results. Automation periods use a separate concurrency pool (default 2, counting only running periods) and a separate queue; they consume no Research slots and never enter the Research Topic FIFO. Queue and slot explanations appear only on the Automations page and in period details. Both section titles are static labels; child items are directly visible and own their navigation. List rows place the name plus type, status and unread labels first. The fixed schedule and time zone follow the title in subdued text, wrapping only when space is constrained. A separate wrapping section below a subtle divider emphasizes Next run and Latest run; activity timestamps remain secondary to the latest outcome. Long names ellipsize on one line and never intrude into the right label area. The all-results list gives each automation a session-level name colour; every row shares one neutral surface and hover, and periods from the same automation share the name colour. The palette is not persisted and only aids grouping in the current session.
- Creation starts by choosing a shape (Quick task / Research automation), then entering one instruction or one-sentence request; choosing a project is no longer required. Both shapes use the same three steps: task → schedule/time zone/three previews → review. Quick's final step is authorization only because its single answer step is derived locally; Research's final step first presents a short research understanding with Refine understanding and Confirm and generate plan actions, then reviews the planner-generated goals before enabling. A fresh form starts from explicit defaults — Daily at 10:00 in the system time zone, Monday–Friday, LatestOnly, minute interval unit and default limits — and switching shape discards any plan approved for the previous shape. Going Back within the same draft keeps its entered values. A fixed bottom action bar shows Back, Save draft and Continue/Enable automation; saving a draft and enabling are two different visual levels. Leaving the creation page while planning keeps a **Planning…** label on the saved draft and a planning notice with a Review planning action on its detail page.
- The detail header places the title, type and status on the left and the execution actions (Pause/Resume schedule, Run manually, Stop this run, Settings; Copy and Archive are not exposed yet) right-aligned on the same row at standard widths. Compact windows wrap the action group beneath the title instead of compressing labels. Status, type, version and owning project share one metadata line below the schedule instead of faking tabs with a chip row. Do not add a second back button: use the top breadcrumb Back, which shows Automations / current automation and returns to the Automations list. All automations and Automation results belong to the separate automation area and do not show Research's All research breadcrumb or a return-to-All-research button. History is a compact, expanded row list with one neutral surface per period and no per-row card border, sorted by the recorded latest-activity timestamp descending. Each period's activity key is its explicit retry request first, otherwise actual start, manual request, scheduled trigger or frozen reference time, with activity kind and ID breaking equal timestamps; a retry can therefore remain below another period after a backward clock change. Each row is already expanded to label the timestamp it uses (Retried, Started, Requested, Scheduled or Reference) plus status, trigger source and Open entry; unread still uses a dot, and rows do not expand further in place. In the all-results list, type and automation name occupy the first line, while timestamp, trigger and status share a wrapping metadata line. The quiet Open icon stays in a separate fixed-width action area, so long names or compact windows cannot push it outside the viewport. Definition history uses the same metadata and action alignment without repeating the automation name. Low-frequency destructive actions such as Cancel period live inside the result dialog details, not in each history row. Inline explanations for queued periods reference only the automation concurrency pool (running / limit), not Research concurrency or queues. Clicking a row opens that period's result in a dialog. Report, sources, a full-title step list with **Continue period** / **Retry period** above it for unfinished work (a failed, cancelled or interrupted period reopens in place with its original approved version and only unfinished steps rerun), and technical details all live in that dialog, technical details are collapsed, and technical details select one run at a time, defaulting to the latest run. Run selectors wrap in compact windows. The selected log scrolls within a viewport-aware height limit: reveal a clipped log first, then scroll only the log until its boundary before handing vertical input to the dialog. Detailed scheduled/start/reference times, time zone, version numbers, and the frozen instruction and expected result live inside Technical details. The default result keeps the header activity time and status, report content, and actionable notices. A single complete report omits the redundant result-state line and generated task heading. Multiple reports keep identifying headings; evidence quality and stale/regenerating/historical warnings remain visible. Do not repeat the original-version label below the version notice. Sources default to a collapsed count disclosure per report. Completed research steps also default to a collapsed progress disclosure; unfinished steps and their Continue/Retry actions remain visible. Sources, research progress, and Technical details share a heading text alignment; source entries, steps, and diagnostics are indented beneath their respective headings. When the current definition differs from the period, keep a notice with guidance to use Run manually for the current version. Definition, expected result, next three triggers, run boundaries (catch-up rule and per-run limits) and the approval snapshot live in the Settings dialog opened from the action row; Edit and Replan sit at the bottom of that dialog next to the settings they change, while the page itself keeps only execution actions. Dialogs use a modal scrim, focus trapping and Escape to close; long content scrolls inside the dialog. Quick periods use the short-answer contract: direct value or conclusion, as-of time, 2–5 sources and at most one or two material caveats; no appendix, methodology, evidence-requirement table, scenario analysis or next steps. Research periods keep the full report contract. Quick period time context in Technical details shows planned/actual start, time zone and reference time; the data window appears only when resolved. Pause and Run manually appear in the same position in every state: grayed out for drafts, with the unlock condition explained below the action row; only drafts cannot run before approval. Scheduled tasks and manual research share one execution path (same agent, working directory and output contract); do not maintain a second agent configuration and do not block enabling by build or by automation. Application-level run preconditions (runs only while the app is open and the computer is awake) sit at the end of the page, not mixed with per-automation information. While paused, Next runs shows a Paused marker and explains that resuming re-schedules, with no catch-up during the pause. Overview body text uses body size and primary color; explanatory text (Quick task description, run limits, disabled-time-zone explanation) uses smaller, muted text, and the two do not share a visual level. The next trigger shows local time only; the time zone already appears in the schedule summary, so do not repeat the abbreviation. Research creates one topic per period; the period topic name appears in the result dialog.
- History starts with 20 rows and **Load more**. The live v4 path requests indexed history pages asynchronously; legacy/offline paths may retain an in-memory presentation limit. Database/read-cost acceptance remains tracked under T14/T15. Unread uses a dot; skipped, failed, running and enabled are separate dimensions, not one status covering all.
- Step execution runs in phase order within each Goal — every collection step, then analysis, then the final report — and each step also waits for its declared prerequisites. A later phase never starts while that Goal's earlier phase is unfinished, even when the reviewed graph would allow it. Goals run in their approved order, so an earlier Goal finishes before the next Goal starts. Only the step whose own attempt failed is shown as Blocked; downstream steps stay Waiting, so the evidence → analysis → report order remains legible. Period and progress rows visibly state the same eligibility phrase, such as Waiting for collection or Queued for automation slot; the reason stays available on the task surface. Automation steps never use Research topic-queue guidance, and definition state remains separate from an active period.
- On storage failure, invalid forms or missing model/project/permission, use a dedicated error area with a reason and never show success. Log cleanup must preview first and state that results/sources are retained.
- Automation-internal Tasks/Topics/Runs are hidden from the normal board, sidebar tree and Research results by default; history results can explicitly open steps, and the breadcrumb returns to that period.
- Inside an automation period, Previous/Next follows only that period's ordered steps: goals in approved order, then workflow order within each goal. Boundaries disable instead of wrapping into another period or automation; a separate Overview action opens the period dialog. Ordinary research task navigation keeps its documented wrapping project-tree order.

## Progressive disclosure

- Daily navigation uses Research results; Settings → Agent & models shows the models used for planning, collection, analysis and reporting.
- Result surfaces use one typed presentation. The entry names **Final report**, **Partial period reports**, **Intermediate evidence**, **Live progress**, **Planning attempt**, **Task result** or **No report**. Evidence quality is a separate fact (**Complete**, **Evidence gaps**, **Needs input**, **Unverified legacy**, **Report rejected**, **Not validated**, **Incomplete**), and freshness labels can coexist (**Stale input**, **Regenerating**, **Original approved version**, **Historical result**, **Legacy result**). Streaming text is never styled as a validated final answer, and a failed period can still expose its finished step results. A result row whose durable body has not been loaded says **saved report** to indicate availability without claiming evidence quality. Opening the reader loads the body; the row then uses the same quality suffix as the reader. Durable quality metadata before first open remains a follow-up; list rendering must not eagerly read every report body.
- The execution page prioritizes task title, status, progress and report. Run history is a compact list; long titles use two lines and reveal the full name on hover. In-progress research protocols never render as reports.
- Technical details keep model, directory, session, time, exit status, Live logs and Saved output. A selected active task shows only one stop button, and batch controls stay visible outside the history list.
- The task page keeps start/view result, status, priority, project and due date by default. More details collapses low-frequency properties and execution settings; missing model or working directory shows a direct fix entry.
- The research page shows goal input and existing research by default; the **Research settings…** label opens the configuration page and input is preserved. Complete Back restoration remains a T10 acceptance item; do not promise it here as already delivered. Direct settings links must open the configuration page.
- Completed research steps use View result as the primary action; rerun is secondary. The goal result entry points to a valid research step and never treats planning JSON as a final report.

All interface icons use [Lucide](https://lucide.dev/icons/) via the pinned gpui-kit assets. Follow [Icon Guidelines](doc/ICON_GUIDELINES.md) for semantic choices, embedding, accessibility, exported reports, and verification. Topics created from another topic use a compact, neutral Copy tag before the title; the detail view retains the source-topic link. In the sidebar, topics keep a uniform 32 px single-line row: Copy and the compact intake status form one leading group before the ellipsized title. The detail header embeds Copy and the full intake status in the first text line. Wrapped title lines return to the full left edge, without reserving a tag column; title selection and copying remain available. Tags use an opaque fill and a thin theme border so their shape remains visible over glass. Sidebar project names omit folder icons to preserve horizontal space. Sidebar group headings use the navigation body size, semibold weight and primary text color to retain hierarchy without indentation. Projects is separated from Research by vertical spacing, without an outer tree border; each project row sits one 12 px step past the group label, and every expanded level (topic, goal, research step) adds the same step so indentation stays uniform. Goal and step rows use the 16 px compact status glyph inside their own inset instead of the 24 px ripple frame, keeping the label column wide for long Chinese titles. Short labels (Aligning, Review, Paused, Attention) preserve title space; tooltips and accessible labels retain the full status meaning. Draft is muted; Understanding and Planning use the existing accent; Needs review and Interrupted use the warning tone; Needs attention uses the danger tone. Planned topics omit this intake status, which is distinct from later research execution status. These tags are passive labels, not separate actions.

- Mermaid previews remain part of normal report scrolling inside a rounded, theme-colored border. SVG is retained as the rendering source so magnification can be rasterized at the requested display size. Math previews and exported report images retain their existing behavior.
- Automation list rows separate fixed schedule rules from live execution information. The schedule and time zone sit beside the title in secondary text, wrapping when necessary; next-run timing and the latest outcome form a distinct wrapping section. Activity timestamps stay subordinate to outcomes, while definition status and unread indicators retain their existing meaning.

- Automation Settings places its schedule beside the title, keeps objective/expected result and upcoming runs above execution details, and presents approved agents/models as neutral tags. Run limits remain visible in concise form; question-mark tooltips carry explanatory prose. Quick tasks omit an empty planning-history section, and available history remains at the bottom. Long tags truncate with a full-value tooltip.

- Automation detail headers place the fixed task-type tag directly after the title, followed by lifecycle status on one wrapping row. The schedule and muted version form a compact secondary row; the task-type tooltip explains its behavior without repeating the Automations destination in body text.

- Read-only agent and model values use neutral passive tags in task bodies, run details and automation Settings. Omit visible field prefixes when context is clear; full values and field names remain available in tooltips and accessible labels. Keep next-run configuration separate from historical run metadata. Editable selectors retain their form labels.

- Automation result dialogs lead with the automation name and report body. Single final reports omit repeated owner/type headings; internal owner identifiers remain in Technical details. Evidence warnings retain prominence, while saved/current version differences use muted metadata with retry semantics in a tooltip.

## Research understanding review

Ordinary Research and Research automation share a compact, selectable research-understanding surface before goal generation. Show the short summary first, followed by at most two material questions. Confirm and generate plan is the primary action; Refine understanding opens a small feedback field. Unanswered questions disable confirmation with a nearby explanation. Generating or saving disables duplicate actions. Active understanding shows progress in the disabled Understand research button instead of a separate repeated progress sentence. Failed understanding generation exposes a retry on the saved request. The run view and request history preserve access after navigation or restart. The current understanding and its actions share one emphasized surface, using standard small body text and a semibold section label at the same size. The confirmation button needs no redundant explanatory sentence when there are no unanswered questions. Topic intake omits the redundant confirmation banner. Goal-opening guidance lives in the goal-count tooltip; Planning details is a quiet action beside the saved request timestamp and status. Clarification history is a quiet disclosure with a round count; expanded history aligns its left edge with the disclosure arrow and lists newest feedback first with compact local timestamps. Each round directly shows its previous understanding, any clarification questions, then user feedback, with semibold field labels. Legacy context uses the same field-label styling. Legacy clarification context follows all structured rounds at the bottom. Entries use zero-based #n markers in a fixed left gutter outside each content rule; the oldest entry is #0. Round numbers are separate from the internal field labels. A successful understanding review omits the duplicate saved-result banner; older combined context remains readable without invented timestamps. Draft autosave keeps the Update understanding button and editor hint stable; only an active request, pending primary submission, or interrupted session blocks updates. Refine understanding saves an unsubmitted draft locally; Close editor preserves it and Continue editing restores it. A nonempty draft prevents confirmation until submitted or cleared. Interrupted understanding exposes Resume understanding after the previous session is confirmed stopped; otherwise show its recovery status and Check session status.
